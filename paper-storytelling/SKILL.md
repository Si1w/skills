# Paper Storytelling

Help readers understand what the study establishes and why the evidence supports it. Work within the requested section or artifact and the project's writing conventions; a local edit does not require rebuilding the paper's narrative.

## Argument and evidence

Identify the intended contribution and the available evidence. Keep observations, interpretations, and untested hypotheses distinct, with claims traceable to results or sources.

Use a figure storyboard when it helps clarify a phenomenon or experimental comparison. Identify the question a proposed panel answers and what observation would weaken the hypothesis. Treat sketches as expectations, not findings; figure-first planning is optional, not a prerequisite for writing.

Arrange claims by their explanatory dependencies. When results challenge the expected story, revise the story and retain counterevidence and qualifications that change the conclusion. Missing evidence limits the affected claim, not improvements elsewhere.

## Figures and prose

Make figures and captions communicate the empirical findings. Use prose to explain the problem, experimental choices, and warranted conclusions. Assess whether each narrative remains understandable on its own at the requested scope.

For figure design, panel arrangement, or a teaser figure, read [figures.md](references/figures.md). It includes optional Python examples; ordinary prose edits do not need them.

## Completion

Deliver the requested revision or storyboard with consequential evidence gaps identified. When producing figures, inspect the rendered output for legibility, clipping, and agreement with the underlying data before delivery.
